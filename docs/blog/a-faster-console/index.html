A faster console: windows that open at once, a table that draws only what you see

Ponos 0.3 makes its console quicker where it counts — the windows, the ticket table, long conversations and the dashboard — with the timings we measured before and after, and the two libraries it now builds on.

Hermez·November 19, 2026·4 min read

Ponos's console is where you watch the backlog move, so a slow click costs more than a slow page. Version 0.3 goes after the four places where it was slow: the windows, the ticket table, long conversations and the dashboard. Here is what changed, and what we measured.

Windows that open at once

Opening a ticket, the palette or New ticket used to hold the page for a while. Two things were to blame: the animations, and the way the table styled itself while a window was open.

  • The side panel, where the conversation lives, slides in within 200 ms at most and closes in 150 ms, instead of 500 and 300 ms. No other exit animation of a component lasts longer than 200 ms.
  • The table and the windows no longer use CSS selectors that forced the browser to restyle the whole page each time a window opened or closed.

On a board of 2,000 tickets, opening or closing a window no longer leaves a single task longer than 50 ms. Before the change, the palette took 74 ms, a 500-message conversation 79 ms, a ticket window 98 ms, and the palette over the ticket list 2.9 s to open with four tasks of about a second to close. The very first opening of New ticket still takes 54 to 64 ms, down from 105 ms: it is the first creation of the Markdown editor, paid once.

A table that draws only what you see

The ticket table now comes from react-resource-view 2.1, which draws only the rows on screen. On a board of 2,000 tickets, the page holds 814 nodes instead of 57,625. A test refuses the selectors that caused the restyling in the built stylesheet, so they do not come back quietly.

The dashboard no longer freezes

On a board of 2,266 tickets, every movement of the table while a ticket ran blocked the page: 850 to 900 ms of script in the feed's update handler, then 640 to 710 ms of rendering, measured on a slow machine (CPU ×4). Now a ticket that did not change keeps its row, its card is not drawn again, and the work is split into pieces. The same movement takes 60 to 100 ms at ×4, in chunks, and no frame of more than 50 ms was seen over a minute without slowdown.

Long conversations stay fast to type in

A conversation of 120 messages made every keystroke wait: the reply field measured its own height on each key, and every message was drawn again with each piece of an answer. Now:

  • the field grows with the browser's own field-sizing: content where it is understood (Chrome, Edge): 34 ms per keystroke became 1 ms on that conversation;
  • messages that are out of view are not laid out, and a message already shown is not drawn again;
  • a conversation opens on its last 60 messages, and a Show earlier button adds 60 more without moving your reading position.

A browser test now fails when a keystroke in a 500-message conversation takes more than 16 ms.

Lighter first load, and a palette that answers

The console's first page in French went from 508 to 455 KB compressed: the words of the settings and the first sign-in come with it, and the pages that need more — a ticket, a project, the ideas — load them when they show.

The Ctrl+K palette filters its own entries instead of letting the library rank every ticket of the board. On a board of 1,848 tickets, the interaction delay while typing five letters went from 1,208–1,288 ms to 88–152 ms.

The two libraries underneath

  • react-resource-view — the console moved from 0.6 to 2.1. The 2.x line brings the virtualised table, and the sticky actions column fixed in 2.0.
  • react-data-form — on 0.3. Its forms are themed by the console's index.css, with the expensive selectors rewritten, so that the scrollbar width removed from the page is no longer inherited by every element, and the page behind a window stops being restyled as a whole.

Nothing changes in how you use the console: the same board, the same shortcuts, the same windows, only quicker to open and to type in.

Try it

Update Ponos to 0.3.0 with your usual command, open a board with a few hundred tickets, and open the palette and a ticket window back to back. The guide's console page describes the views, and the installation guide covers an update.
